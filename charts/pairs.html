ENHC — The 8,8 vs A Cell

The one ENHC override on this chart: 8,8 vs A is Hit, not Split. Splitting risks losing both bets to a dealer natural, which dominates the upside.

Under OBO (original-bet-only on dealer BJ), this reverts to Split. Confirm at the table — the rule varies by property.

DAS Drives the Low Pairs

2,2 / 3,3 vs 2–3 and 6,6 vs 2 only become splits because double-after-split is allowed. Without DAS, all three flip to Hit.

4,4 vs 5–6 is the most DAS-dependent cell — without DAS it's a clear Hit.

The 9,9 vs 7 Trap

Looks like an obvious split — it's not. Dealer 7 is most likely to make a 17, and your 18 already beats that. Stand and collect.

Same logic for 9,9 vs 10 and 9,9 vs A: the dealer's strong upcard makes 18 worth keeping.

5,5 and 10,10 — Never Split

Two pairs you treat as their total instead. 5,5 plays as hard 10 (so Double vs 2–9). 10,10 stays as a winning 20 — splitting it is the most expensive amateur mistake in the game.

Composite Edge — All Three Charts

Soft + Hard + Pairs together is the complete basic strategy under Macau rules. Executed perfectly, the house edge sits near 0.15% at OBO properties (Grand Lisboa, MGM) and near 0.25% at full-ENHC properties (Galaxy, Venetian). For context: that's roughly half the edge of a typical Vegas Strip 6:5 game, and one-tenth the casino's actual hold against the average Macau blackjack player who declines to split, double, or surrender on instinct.
